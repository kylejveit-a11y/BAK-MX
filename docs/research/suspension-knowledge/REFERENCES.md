## Recommendations from the survey

- Suspension Bible: buy 1-2 print copies (US$39.99 list) for the human reviewer or subject-matter expert. Use it to author and audit BAK MX's own rule set (symptom to direction of change, what to check first, test method), written in our own words. Do not scan, OCR, quote at length or feed it to the model. If the AI must draw directly on its content, or the app wants to carry Race Tech's name or endorsement, email Race Tech (info@racetech.com, 951-279-6655) proposing a content or partnership licence. Race Tech may control the seminar IP. Also contact Quarto/Motorbooks (rights via the quarto.com Contact/Copyright pages) for text rights, and ask specifically about AI/RAG use. Until a licence is signed it is app_use=reviewer_reference.
- Free substitutes for the Bible's practical content: the Race Tech sag page (method), the Teknik Offroad Setup Guide and Transmoto 'Using Your Clickers' (symptom tables, clicker basics), Keefer 'Helpful Suspension Set Up Tips' (test methodology, air forks), MXA's two 'Ten Things' articles (check-first items, 'soft is stiff'), and the Honda/KTM/Yamaha owner's-manual tuning chapters (OEM direction-of-change guidance). Between them they cover sag, clicker direction, symptom tables, spring-by-weight logic and test method.
- For every 'cite_and_summarise' source, store only our own paraphrased rule plus source id, URL and access date, not the page text. Show a 'Source:' link in the app. Every site checked shows 'All rights reserved', so whole-page ingestion into a vector store needs permission, even for free pages.
- OEM manuals: treat them as the authoritative source for bike-specific numbers (stock clicker settings, sag spec, spring rates, fork oil level), with model year and market noted. KTM explicitly forbids reproduction 'even in part' without written permission, so present numbers as cited facts and paraphrase procedures. Do not copy tables or diagrams. Have counsel confirm, and consider asking OEMs (KTM, Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki) for written permission to display spec values.
- Get manuals only from official sources (cdn.powersports.honda.com, Yamaha distributor sites or yamahapubs.com, KTM/Husqvarna/GasGas official portals or dealers, Kawasaki parts counter). Block the many pirate and spam 'free manual' domains in any crawler.
- Keep tester baselines (Keefer, MXA, Racer X) out of the app's stated numbers, since they are not OEM-verified. Use them only for human sanity checks, or as clearly labelled third-party opinions with a link if product policy allows.
- E-MX versus gas: most sources assume a gas bike with engine braking, a fuel load that changes over a moto, and gas-bike mass. Flag guidance that depends on these (e.g. rear squat on decel, sag-to-balance with battery mass, Varg pro-stiff stock valving). Write e-MX caveat rules from the Charged Cycle Works piece and future Stark/KTM SX-E manuals.
- Dating risk: the Bible (2010), Gorr (2004), Bell and Jennings predate modern air forks (WP XACT, Showa TAC, KYB PSF) and EFI mapping apps. Prefer 2020+ magazine and OEM sources for air-fork pressure guidance, and check older rules against them.
- Brand-bias risk: Race Tech, Öhlins, Teknik and Charged Cycle Works all sell products or services. Have the app recommend free adjustments first (sag, clickers, tire pressure, fork height, linkage maintenance) before any part purchase.
- Engineering books (Foale, Cossalter, Bradley, Dixon) are reviewer-only references for checking physics claims (geometry, leverage ratios, damper force-velocity behaviour). They are not worth licensing for rider-facing output.
- Follow-ups not completed: yamahapubs.com and motochassis.com were blocked during research. Verify Yamaha US manual terms and Foale's edition and price directly. Also check WP's own XACT setup documentation and Showa/KYB official sites, since no free official WP/KYB/Showa rider setup guide was found in search.
