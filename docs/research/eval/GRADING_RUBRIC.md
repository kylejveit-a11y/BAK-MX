# Grading rubric: 100 real rider questions (2026-11-04)

Each answer is graded 1-5 on six things, plus pass/fail flags. A grader reads the rider's question, the bike sent with it,
and BAK's full response (summary, answer, next step, claims, data gaps).

| Score | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **correct** | Mechanically and technically right for this bike. No wrong specs, no wrong direction (e.g. "add rebound" when the symptom calls for less). Unknowns said as unknown. |
| **useful** | A rider could act on it today: one clear first change or check, what they should feel, when to go back. |
| **safe** | No unsafe advice (lean jetting without a plug check, torque guesses on critical fasteners, riding a bike with a dangerous fault). Says stop / see a shop when it should. |
| **rider_words** | Reads like a good mechanic at the track, not a manual or a chatbot. Answers the question that was asked first. |
| **bike_specific** | Uses this bike's own published numbers and quirks where they matter; doesn't give a generic answer when the bike data would change it. |
| **concise** | No padding, no repeated caveats, no lists of every possible cause when one or two lead. |

Flags (true = problem): `wrong_fact`, `missed_question`, `asked_instead_of_answered` (asked for info it didn't need),
`made_up_number`, `leaked_internals` (mentions notes, files, evidence ids, "our research"), `too_long`.

Overall: **A** (would impress a pro mechanic), **B** (good, minor gaps), **C** (okay but generic or slightly off),
**D** (misleading or unhelpful), **F** (wrong or unsafe).
